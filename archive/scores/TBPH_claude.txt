(a) Phase 3 Success in nOH / Dysautonomia
──────────────────────────────────────────
• Droxidopa (Northera): Approved 2014 for nOH. Three Phase 3 studies
  had MIXED results. Study 301: met primary. Study 302: met primary
  on enriched population only. Study 303 (long-term withdrawal):
  did NOT meet primary endpoint. FDA approval was controversial and
  narrow (short-term treatment only). Durability unproven.
  [Source: PMC/Expert Review Cardiovasc Therapy, PMC4509799]
• Midodrine: Approved for OH (not specifically nOH). Historical.
• Atomoxetine (NRI, off-label): Some efficacy signals in nOH but
  not formally developed for this indication.
• Ampreloxetine prior Phase 3 (REDWOOD, 2021): FAILED. Did not meet
  primary endpoint in broad nOH population (PD + MSA + PAF). Led to
  75% workforce reduction at Theravance.
  [Source: Fierce Biotech, March 3, 2026; MedCity News, March 3, 2026]
• Ampreloxetine was THIRD Phase 3 failure (2021 REDWOOD broad, 2021
  REDWOOD MSA subgroup analysis prompted CYPRESS, now CYPRESS failed).
  [Source: Clinical Trials Arena, March 4, 2026]

  BASE RATE (Phase 3 in nOH): ~25-30% (historically very difficult)

(b) Randomized Withdrawal Design in nOH
────────────────────────────────────────
• CYPRESS used enrichment/withdrawal: 20-week open-label treatment,
  then 8-week randomized withdrawal comparing ampreloxetine vs
  placebo continuation.
• This design is biased IN FAVOR of the drug — only patients who
  respond during open-label are randomized. Despite this enrichment,
  the trial still failed.
• Droxidopa also used randomized withdrawal designs and had mixed
  results even with this favorable design.
• The PRO endpoint (OHSA composite) is highly subjective and subject
  to placebo/nocebo effects, expectation bias during withdrawal.

  BASE RATE (RW design, enriched, nOH): ~30-35%

(c) Ampreloxetine-Specific Data
────────────────────────────────
• Phase 2 (n=34, PMC8629777): Small, mostly open-label. Part B
  (double-blind, n=10) showed significant SBP increase vs placebo
  (29.9 mmHg difference, p=0.0112). Part C (open-label extension,
  n=21): 3.1-point improvement in OHSA#1, standing BP increased
  ~11 mmHg. Symptoms returned to baseline after withdrawal.
  [Source: Clin Auton Research, PMC8629777]
• Pressor effect confirmed but SYMPTOM improvement was modest and
  measured primarily in open-label extension (high bias risk).
• Blood pressure improvement does NOT always translate to symptom
  improvement in nOH — this has been the recurring lesson in the field.
• Only 12 of 21 patients completed week 20 in Phase 2. Dropout was
  substantial, especially among MSA patients (rapid disease progression).

  BASE RATE (ampreloxetine-specific, post-Phase 2): ~25-35%

COMPOSITE BASE RATE: ~28%

FACTOR 1: Prior Data Strength (Weight: 30%)
────────────────────────────────────────────
Score: 3.5/10

POSITIVES:
• Phase 2 showed real biological activity — BP increased, NE levels
  confirmed. Mechanism of action is pharmacologically sound.
• Symptom improvement during open-label was meaningful (3.1 points
  on OHSA#1 from baseline of 6.6).
• Symptoms returned to baseline after withdrawal — suggests the
  benefit was drug-specific, not placebo.
• Once-daily dosing (vs TID for droxidopa) is a genuine advantage.

NEGATIVES (SEVERE):
• Phase 2 double-blind portion was only N=10. Grossly underpowered.
• Phase 2 symptom data came primarily from OPEN-LABEL extension —
  massive bias risk in a subjective PRO endpoint.
• Phase 2 pressor effect (SBP increase) faded within 8-12 hours in
  acute setting (Part B). Only standing BP showed modest sustained
  increase in extension.
• Prior Phase 3 (2021 REDWOOD) FAILED in broad nOH population.
  MSA subgroup post hoc analysis was the basis for CYPRESS — this
  is a red flag (post hoc subgroup-driven pivotal design).
• High dropout rate in Phase 2 (only 12/21 completed week 20).

WEIGHTED CONTRIBUTION: 3.5 × 0.30 = 1.05

────────────────────────────────────────────────
FACTOR 2: Trial Design Risk (Weight: 30%)
────────────────────────────────────────────────
Score: 4.0/10

POSITIVES:
• Randomized withdrawal design favors drug (enrichment).
• FDA-agreed design after REDWOOD failure — regulatory alignment.
• MSA-focused population based on hypothesis of more intact nerves.
• n=102 reasonable for rare disease.

NEGATIVES:
• OHSA composite is a SUBJECTIVE patient-reported outcome. In MSA
  patients with cognitive and motor decline, PRO reliability is
  questionable.
• MSA-only enrollment: These patients have progressive, rapidly
  worsening neurodegeneration. 20-week open-label + 8-week withdrawal
  means 28+ weeks of disease progression during the study.
• Randomized withdrawal may cause nocebo effect — patients know they
  might be taken off drug, potentially worsening self-reported symptoms
  in BOTH arms (reducing detectable difference).
• Small n=102 for a heterogeneous MSA population with high variability
  in autonomic dysfunction severity.
• 8-week withdrawal period may be too short or too long to detect
  meaningful symptom rebound.

WEIGHTED CONTRIBUTION: 4.0 × 0.30 = 1.20

────────────────────────────────────────────────
FACTOR 3: Disease Biology / Therapeutic Challenge (Weight: 20%)
────────────────────────────────────────────────
Score: 3.0/10

POSITIVES:
• NET inhibition is pharmacologically rational for nOH — potentiates
  residual norepinephrine at the synapse.
• In MSA, preganglionic lesions theoretically spare more postganglionic
  fibers than in PD/PAF — more NE available for NET inhibition.
• Once-daily oral convenience is genuinely differentiated.

NEGATIVES:
• nOH in MSA is driven by PROGRESSIVE central neurodegeneration.
  NET inhibition can only work if there's residual NE to potentiate.
  As MSA progresses, residual sympathetic tone diminishes.
• The BP-to-symptom disconnect is fundamental: droxidopa raises BP
  but durability of symptom improvement has never been established
  beyond 1-2 weeks in controlled settings.
• MSA patients have diverse and rapidly evolving symptoms — nOH is
  just one of many debilitating features. Isolating nOH symptom
  improvement from global functional decline is very difficult.
• The field has seen repeated failures: multiple Phase 3 attempts
  for nOH drugs have failed over the past 15 years.

WEIGHTED CONTRIBUTION: 3.0 × 0.20 = 0.60

RATIONALE FOR LOW PRE-EVENT ESTIMATE (25%):
─────────────────────────────────────────────
1. SERIAL FAILURE: Ampreloxetine had ALREADY failed a Phase 3 in 2021
   (REDWOOD). The CYPRESS study was a rescue attempt based on a POST
   HOC subgroup analysis of MSA patients. Post hoc-driven pivotal
   studies have historically high failure rates.
   [Source: Fierce Biotech, March 3, 2026]

2. nOH IS AN EXTREMELY DIFFICULT THERAPEUTIC AREA: The disconnect
   between blood pressure improvement and symptom improvement is
   fundamental. Droxidopa was controversially approved with mixed
   Phase 3 data and has never demonstrated durable symptom benefit.
   [Source: PMC4509799, Expert Rev Cardiovasc Ther]

3. MSA DISEASE PROGRESSION: MSA patients worsen rapidly. A 28-week
   study span means significant neurological deterioration confounding
   symptom assessment. The "signal" from NET inhibition may be
   overwhelmed by disease progression noise.

4. SUBJECTIVE PRO ENDPOINT: OHSA composite relies entirely on
   patient self-report in a cognitively declining population. This
   is inherently noisy and vulnerable to expectation/nocebo effects.

5. PHASE 2 WAS UNDERPOWERED AND PRIMARILY OPEN-LABEL: The controlled
   data (n=10) was inadequate to predict Phase 3 success. Open-label
   symptom improvement in a highly symptomatic population is unreliable.
   [Source: Clin Auton Research, PMC8629777]

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
POST-MORTEM ANALYSIS
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
The CYPRESS failure confirmed the pre-event skepticism:
• Primary endpoint (OHSA composite at Week 8 withdrawal) was NOT
  statistically significant.
• Secondary endpoints showed similar non-significant trends.
• HOWEVER: BP, heart rate, and NE levels confirmed biological
  activity — the drug WORKS pharmacologically, it just doesn't
  translate to symptom relief in a controlled setting.
  [Source: PR Newswire/Theravance, March 3, 2026]

This is the classic nOH paradox: you can move biomarkers (BP, NE)
but cannot reliably move subjective symptoms in a controlled trial.
The disease is too complex, the endpoints too noisy, and the
underlying neurodegeneration too dominant.

Company response: Wind down ampreloxetine, cut 50% workforce,
strategic review (potential sale). Remaining value is ~$400M cash,
YUPELRI royalties, TRELEGY milestone.
[Source: Yahoo Finance, March 4, 2026; Fierce Biotech, March 3, 2026]
