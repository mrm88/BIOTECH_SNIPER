CRITICAL CONTEXT: The upcoming readout is the 16-week extension data, NOT the
primary endpoint. The primary endpoint was already reported (Dec 2025). This

FACTOR 1: Prior Phase 2b Data Quality (Weight: 25%)
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Score: 5.5/10

POSITIVES:
+ 36-week data showed clear dose-dependent separation from placebo
+ Both dose arms showed ~30% mean SALT reduction vs 6% placebo (modified pop)
+ Consistent with Phase 1b AD data (72% BSA improvement, durable 36+ wks)
+ Late-breaking oral presentation at AAD 2026 → peer recognition of data quality
+ Safety profile excellent: 1.4% discontinuation, no MACE/thrombosis
+ Phase 2b in AD met primary + ALL key secondary endpoints → cross-indication validation
+ REZOLVE-AD advancing to Phase 3 (ZENITH-AD) in Q2 2026 → company confidence
+ Biomarker data: 6-fold Treg expansion; correlated Th2 marker suppression
+ FDA granted Fast Track in both AD and AA

NEGATIVES:
- PRIMARY ENDPOINT MISSED on ITT analysis (p=0.186 and p=0.121)
- Required exclusion of 4 patients for stat sig (post-hoc; p=0.049, p=0.042)
- p-values even in modified analysis were MARGINAL (barely significant)
- n=92 is VERY small trial — high variance, fragile statistics
- 28% mean SALT reduction is modest compared to JAK inhibitors
- No specific SALT50 or SALT≤20 response rates have been disclosed publicly
- "Proof of concept" language from company = hedging, not declaring victory

ASSESSMENT: The data are MIXED. There IS biological activity and clinical signal,
but the ITT miss is a real negative. The small sample size cuts both ways — it
explains the p-value miss but also means the effect size estimate is noisy.
The 4-patient exclusion is scientifically defensible (enrollment violations) but
will face regulatory scrutiny. The AD data (393 patients, clean hit) provides
important cross-indication support.

FACTOR 2: Mechanism Novelty and Biological Plausibility (Weight: 15%)
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Score: 7/10

POSITIVES:
+ First-in-class Treg stimulator — entirely novel mechanism for AA
+ AA is a T-cell-mediated autoimmune disease → Tregs directly address pathology
+ Strong preclinical rationale: Treg deficiency/dysfunction documented in AA
+ REZPEG demonstrated 6-fold Treg expansion + suppression of Th2 markers in vivo
+ 12-17x increase in CD25bright Tregs (most suppressive subset) in Phase 1
+ "Resolution therapeutic" — addresses root cause, not downstream cytokine
+ Durability signal: Phase 1b AD showed effects lasting 36 weeks post-treatment
+ No induction of conventional T-effector cells (selectivity maintained)

NEGATIVES:
- Treg-based therapies have NEVER produced an approved autoimmune drug
- Biological activity (Treg expansion) has not reliably translated to clinical outcomes
- AA may require more potent/direct immunosuppression than Treg modulation provides
- The modest effect size (28-30% SALT reduction) suggests mechanism may be insufficient
  vs JAK inhibitors (which achieve 35-41% SALT≤20 rates in 24-36 wks)

ASSESSMENT: Strong biological plausibility with clean on-target PD. But the
translational gap from "Tregs expand" to "hair regrows" is real. JAK inhibitors
have a more direct mechanism (block cytokine signaling to hair follicle) whereas
Tregs must indirectly restore immune tolerance. Novelty is high; certainty of
clinical translation is moderate.

FACTOR 3: Endpoint Achievability vs. Historical Bar (Weight: 20%)
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Score: 6/10

CONTEXT FOR APRIL READOUT:
The April readout is the 16-week EXTENSION (Week 36→52), not the primary endpoint.
Patients eligible for extension: those who showed hair growth but hadn't reached
SALT>20 by Week 36. This is a SELECTED population of partial responders.

The question: Do these partial responders convert to deeper responses?

Historical context on response deepening:
- Baricitinib: Response continued to improve from Wk36→Wk52 and beyond
  - SALT≤20 rates increased from ~39% (Wk36) to higher at Wk52
  - "Early, gradual, late" responder patterns identified; some respond after Wk36
- Ritlecitinib: SALT≤20 went from 23% (Wk24) → 43% (Wk48) → 60-63% (Mo24)
  - Clear pattern of continued response deepening over time
- Deuruxolitinib: 32.6% response at Wk24 → 76.6% at Wk68
- REZPEG in AD: Maintenance period showed continued/deepening responses
- REZPEG in AA: "Majority of patients experienced hair growth at Week 16 or later"
  - Suggests later onset consistent with Treg mechanism (slower than JAK)

POSITIVES:
+ Historical precedent for AA response deepening with continued treatment is strong
+ REZPEG's Treg mechanism has inherently slower onset → more upside from extension
+ Patients in extension are SELECTED partial responders → enriched for drug effect
+ Phase 1b AD showed durable and deepening response over time
+ Extension is blinded → no placebo unblinding bias

NEGATIVES:
- Extension population is small (subset of already-small n=92 trial)
- Extension is NOT a pre-specified powered analysis — exploratory
- Some responders may plateau rather than deepen
- 16 additional weeks may not be enough for Treg mechanism to fully manifest
- If response deepening is modest, it's a neutral-to-negative read for Phase 3

ASSESSMENT: The bar is relatively LOW for this extension. It's not a binary
hit/miss — it's a directional read. Any meaningful deepening of response or
additional SALT≤20 conversions would be positive. The risk is that responses
plateau, which would be interpreted negatively by the market.

FACTOR 4: Patient Population Selection/Enrichment (Weight: 15%)
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Score: 6/10

POSITIVES:
+ Severe-to-very-severe (SALT≥50) ensures measurable disease and room for improvement
+ Very severe (SALT≥95) capped at 25% — avoids dilution by hardest-to-treat patients
+ JAK inhibitor-naïve and biologic-naïve population → no prior treatment failures
+ Stable disease required (6 months) → reduces spontaneous remission noise
+ Extension population is enriched: only partial responders (showed growth but <SALT20)
+ Stratification by baseline severity

NEGATIVES:

  - Placebo response can vary by geography (the 1 placebo eligibility violator

- n=92 overall, likely n=30-50 in extension → very small
- No biomarker selection for Treg responsiveness
- Disease duration not capped → long-duration patients may be less responsive

ASSESSMENT: Reasonable enrichment but the geographic concentration and small
sample size are weaknesses. Extension population being enriched for partial
responders is favorable.

FACTOR 5: Competitive Context and Differentiation (Weight: 10%)
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Score: 7.5/10

COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE:
- Baricitinib (Olumiant, LLY): JAK1/2 — approved; boxed warning for MACE/thrombosis
- Ritlecitinib (Litfulo, PFE): JAK3/TEC — approved; also carries JAK class warnings
- Deuruxolitinib (Leqvi, SUN): JAK1/2 — approved 2024; similar safety profile
- Dupilumab (Dupixent, REGN): Anti-IL-4Rα — marginal AA efficacy; approved for AD

REZPEG DIFFERENTIATION:
+ First non-JAK mechanism if approved → addresses entire class safety concern
+ JAK inhibitors carry boxed warnings: MACE, VTE, malignancy, serious infections
+ Prescribing burden of JAK inhibitors: lab monitoring, risk discussion, REMS
+ Many dermatologists and patients avoid JAK inhibitors due to safety profile
+ Biologic (injectable) → different patient/prescriber segments than oral JAKs
+ "No increased risk of MACE, thrombosis, infection, acne, or oral herpes" per trial
+ Could expand treatable population to patients who refuse JAK risk
+ First-line biologic positioning if efficacy supports it

NEGATIVES:
- Efficacy bar set HIGH by JAK inhibitors (35-41% SALT≤20 at 24-36 weeks)
- REZPEG 28-30% mean SALT reduction may not translate to competitive SALT≤20 rates
- 3 approved oral drugs already → late entrant needs differentiated profile
- Would be injectable vs oral → convenience disadvantage for some patients
- If dupilumab (also injectable) failed to impress, investors are skeptical of non-JAK

ASSESSMENT: The differentiation story is genuinely compelling IF efficacy is
adequate. Safety advantage is clear and clinically meaningful. The risk is that
REZPEG is "safer but less effective" — a tough commercial position.

FACTOR 6: Trial Design Quality (Weight: 15%)
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Score: 5/10

POSITIVES:
+ Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled (gold standard)
+ Two active dose arms + placebo → dose-response evaluation
+ 36-week primary treatment period (appropriate for AA)
+ Stratified randomization by disease severity
+ Blinded extension period (maintains blinding integrity)
+ Secondary endpoints well-chosen (SALT≤30/20/10, ClinRO EB/EL)

NEGATIVES:
- n=92 is UNDERPOWERED for a Phase 2b efficacy trial
  (Compare: ALLEGRO n=718, BRAVE-AA n=1200, THRIVE-AA n=706+517)
- Primary endpoint was MEAN SALT reduction (continuous), not SALT≤20 (binary)
  - This is a less standard and lower bar than Phase 3 registrational endpoints
  - FDA typically requires binary responder rates (SALT≤20) for approval
- 3:3:2 randomization → only ~23 patients per treatment arm, ~23 placebo
  - Single patient outlier in placebo changed statistical outcome (!!!)
- No pre-specified analysis excluding eligibility violations
  - Post-hoc exclusion is scientifically defensible but statistically problematic
- Extension period is OPTIONAL and not powered for efficacy
- 62% enrollment in Poland — geographic concentration risk

ASSESSMENT: The trial design is adequate for proof-of-concept but has real
statistical fragility. A single patient swung the ITT analysis. The small n
means any April extension data will have wide confidence intervals. This is
a Phase 2b designed for signal detection, not definitive efficacy — which is
appropriate for its stage, but means the data can be spun either direction.

UPSIDE DRIVERS:
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
1. AA RESPONSE DEEPENING IS THE NORM: Every approved AA drug showed continued
   improvement beyond initial treatment period. Ritlecitinib went from 23% → 43%
   → 60%+ SALT≤20. If REZPEG follows this pattern, the extension data will look
   impressive, and the "slow onset Treg mechanism" narrative becomes a feature.

2. CROSS-INDICATION VALIDATION IS STRONG: REZOLVE-AD (n=393) hit primary and
   ALL key secondary endpoints. Phase 3 ZENITH-AD initiating Q2 2026. This is
   the strongest evidence that REZPEG works in T-cell-driven skin disease. If the
   mechanism works in AD, it has a higher prior probability of working in AA.

3. FIRST NON-JAK BIOLOGIC FOR AA: Massive differentiation opportunity. JAK boxed
   warnings are real barriers — dermatologists want an alternative. If REZPEG shows
   adequate efficacy, the safety advantage alone could drive a large market share.

4. SHORT SQUEEZE SETUP: 17.4% short interest, ~$57 stock price (up from $0.60-0.80
   in early 2025). Heavy shorts + binary catalyst = potential squeeze event. The
   stock has already re-rated significantly on AD data but AA extension success
   would provide another leg up.

5. FDA ENGAGEMENT: Dual Fast Track designations (AD and AA). End-of-Phase-2 FDA
   meeting planned for Q2 2026 in AA. FDA is telegraphing willingness to engage
   on this mechanism and indication.

DOWNSIDE RISKS:
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
1. THE ITT ANALYSIS MISSED: The primary endpoint of REZOLVE-AA did NOT meet
   statistical significance in the intent-to-treat population (p=0.186). This
   is a fact. The post-hoc exclusion of 4 patients is defensible but fragile.
   Regulatory agencies and sophisticated investors will weigh this heavily.

2. n=92 IS DANGEROUSLY SMALL: With ~35 patients per treatment arm and ~23 on
   placebo, a single outlier swung the entire result. The extension cohort is
   even smaller (subset of partial responders). Any data from this extension
   will have massive confidence intervals and limited statistical power.

3. TREG MECHANISM IS UNPROVEN IN AUTOIMMUNE: Zero approved Treg-based drugs
   for any autoimmune indication. Low-dose IL-2 has missed primary endpoints
   in SLE trials despite robust Treg expansion. "Tregs go up" ≠ "disease improves."
   REZPEG may prove to be the drug that breaks the pattern, but the class has
   consistently disappointed.

4. EFFECT SIZE MAY BE INSUFFICIENT VS. JAK INHIBITORS: 28-30% mean SALT 
   reduction at 36 weeks is likely well below JAK inhibitors on the standard
   SALT≤20 registration endpoint. If Phase 3 head-to-head comparisons ever
   emerge, REZPEG could look inferior. Commercial viability requires "good enough"
   efficacy — unclear if this reaches the bar.

5. COMPANY RISK: Nektar lost $164M in FY2025 on $55M revenue. Cash runway is
   reportedly to 2027. Phase 3 in both AD and AA is enormously expensive. The
   company may need to raise capital (dilutive) or partner (value-sharing).
   If the AA extension disappoints, it weakens the overall REZPEG thesis and
   makes financing harder.
